\subsection{Replicate variance on the ablation grid}
\label{sec:tier-noise}

We ran the leave-one-out ablation over three model tiers on
\textsc{subset20b}, a 20-task split of LakeQA stratified jointly on node count
and historical solve rate. Every axis is held at the oracle and one axis is
degraded per cell, giving seven cells per model. To establish whether the
resulting drops are measurable at this sample size, the two smaller tiers were
run a second time under identical configuration.

The replicate is the central result. Across the 14 paired cells the mean
absolute difference between runs is \textbf{7.1\,pp} (median 5.0\,pp, maximum
20.0\,pp). Since a single task is worth 5\,pp at $n=20$, this places the
resolution of the design near 15--20\,pp rather than the 9--16\,pp range the
ablation was expected to produce. Table~\ref{tab:tier-ablation} reports both
runs; Figure~\ref{fig:tier-noise} plots each effect against the noise floor.

Three consequences follow. First, the apparent advantage of \textsc{Preloaded}
over the all-ideal reference ($+10$\,pp at \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano} and $+15$\,pp
at \texttt{gpt-5-mini} on run~1 alone) does not survive replication, falling to
$+5.0$ and $+7.5$\,pp respectively. Second, one cell moved 20\,pp between
identical runs (\texttt{gpt-5.4-nano}, \textsc{Plan: No Plan}, 25\%~$\to$~45\%),
so no single-run reading of that condition is interpretable. Third, only two
effects clear the floor by a comfortable margin: the search axis at
$-20$\,pp for BM25 at \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano} and $-20$\,pp for PNEUMA at
\texttt{gpt-5-mini}.

\texttt{gpt-5.2} was run once and therefore carries no error bar. Its search
axis is notably flat ($-5$\,pp for BM25, against $-10$ to $-20$\,pp for the
smaller tiers), which would indicate that retrieval quality matters less as
model capability rises; we report it as an observation rather than a finding,
since a single run at $n=20$ cannot separate it from the variance measured on
the other two tiers.
